# Roles and access

Access follows the way the Unit is organized: the country is divided into regions, each region into districts, and each district holds schools. Everyone sees only the part of that tree they're responsible for.

| Role | Scope | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **System administrator** | Every region and school | Adds directors and assigns them to regions; can act anywhere |
| **Director** | Schools in their own region | Manages schools, academic years, terms and subjects; assigns heads; sees regional reports and the audit log |
| **Head** (and assistant head) | One school | Manages staff, classes and students; approves teachers' requests and results |
| **Form teacher** | Their own class | Owns the class: sees all its marks, coordinates exams and takes the register |
| **Support teacher** | Their assigned subjects | Records scores for the subjects they teach, in the classes they teach them |

Form teacher and support teacher aren't separate accounts. Every teacher has the teacher role, and a teacher becomes a form teacher or support teacher through their assignments for the year. One person can be the form teacher of one class and teach a subject in several others.

## How scoping works

- A director's lists of schools, people, staff assignments and audit entries are filtered to their region. Schools are broken down by district within it.
- A head works inside their own school, and a teacher inside the classes and subjects assigned to them.
- Teachers can't change student records directly. They submit a request, and a head approves or rejects it; see [Approvals]({% link approvals.md %}).

## Accounts

New teacher accounts are created by a head or director, and the teacher chooses their own password the first time they sign in.
